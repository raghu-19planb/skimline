Methods, sources and limits

Skimline is a planning aid built entirely from open data. This page states what it measures, where each number comes from, and what it cannot know. It is not for flight operations.

The question

Water-scooping aircraft such as the Canadair CL-415 refill by skimming a lake or reservoir. How many loads an aircraft delivers in an hour depends mostly on how far the fire is from water it can use. Two things decide whether a water body is usable: it must offer a long enough straight run, and that run must still be there when the fire comes. Reservoirs shrink in the same droughts that bring large fires.

What already exists

This project builds on earlier work and should be read alongside it.

  • RAND (Keating et al., 2012, MG-1234) estimated how many historical US fires lay near scooper-accessible water. It did not consider depth or seasonal change.
  • Wotton and Jurko (Natural Resources Canada, for the Swiss Federal Office for the Environment, 2025) screened Swiss lakes against CL-415 size criteria. Static, no drought dimension.
  • TracPlus Labs (2026) screened about 5.7 million US water bodies by geometry. It states that it does not assess depth or current water level.
  • Agencies hold their own knowledge. British Columbia cites more than 1,700 skimmable water bodies; Catalonia assesses reservoirs by reconnaissance flight; the Valencian region publishes an open water-point layer that flags seaplane loading.

We found no public, open map of scooper-suitable water for Canada or Spain as a whole, and no study that follows the loss of refill sites through a drought over time. The effect itself is documented: in April 2023 Catalan fire service amphibious aircraft could no longer use the La Baells and La Llosa del Cavall reservoirs at about 26 % of capacity (3Cat, 1 April 2023), and a US interagency safety bulletin (IA APB 21-05, 2021) warns that approved scoop sites must be reassessed in drought.

Step 1 — the longest straight run in each water body

For every lake and reservoir polygon, the pipeline finds the longest straight line that stays in the water with a clear corridor around it. It tries every direction in 3° steps, slices the polygon with parallel lines, and keeps the longest stretch where the full corridor width is water. Islands break the run. The method is unit-tested against shapes with known answers.

Rivers are excluded in this version. Some Spanish canyon reservoirs are mapped as river sections in OpenStreetMap and are therefore missing; replacing the source with the official national reservoir inventory is on the roadmap.

Step 2 — minutes per load from any point

The map is divided into 1.5 km cells. For each cell the model finds the nearest usable run and converts the straight-line distance to minutes per load with the US Forest Service rule of thumb for flat terrain. Loads per hour is 60 divided by minutes per load; litres per hour multiplies by the tank volume.

Step 3 — what the satellite saw

A mapped reservoir outline is usually its full level. For the largest reservoirs, the pipeline reads Sentinel-2 imagery at 10 m for every month since 2021 and measures the water that was actually there. A pixel inside the mapped outline counts as water if the scene classification says water, or either of two water indices does (NDWI above 0, MNDWI above 0.1). Isolated single-pixel gaps are filled. Up to three of the clearest scenes are measured each month and the wettest is kept, because water does not come and go within a month but haze and glint do hide it. Scenes with more than 3 % cloud, shadow or missing pixels over the reservoir are skipped. The run is then re-measured inside the observed water.

Check: in 2024, a wet year, the observed run of the two largest reservoirs stayed within about 6 % of the run measured on their mapped outlines in the months they were full. A formal validation against gauge levels is on the roadmap.

Test against a documented case, and what it shows

On 1 April 2023 the Catalan fire service said its amphibious aircraft could no longer use the La Baells reservoir, then at about 26 % of capacity, because it lacked a safe enough water surface. Helicopters still could. This is the best-documented case we found, so we ran the satellite step on it.

  • The satellite saw the drawdown. On 2 April 2023 the water covered 42 % of La Baells' mapped outline, down from 55 % the previous autumn.
  • The run length alone did not predict the closure. The longest straight run measured that day was about 2,120 m, still above the 1,852 m default. By geometry alone the reservoir would have counted as usable.
  • At the second reservoir the run length did agree. La Llosa del Cavall was closed to the same aircraft that week. On 7 April 2023 its longest straight run measured about 930 m, with water over 35 % of the mapped outline. That is well short of any published requirement.

One case each way. The lesson is that a long enough run is necessary but not sufficient. What closed La Baells was almost certainly depth and hazards exposed by the falling water, which open outlines cannot show. The site therefore reports the water surface share beside the run, and treats a sharp fall in surface as a warning even where the run still looks long enough. Turning surface loss into a depth estimate, using reservoir level records, is the next step on the roadmap.

What this cannot tell you

  • Depth. A long run over shallow water is not usable. Drawdown exposes stumps, bars and old structures.
  • Obstacles and approach. Power lines, bridges, boats, and terrain on the approach and climb-out are not checked.
  • Permission. Whether an agency or dam operator has approved a site is not public.
  • Altitude and heat. The required run grows with elevation and temperature. The slider lets you set it; the map does not yet do so per lake.
  • Other aircraft. Helicopters and single-engine scoopers need far less water and are not modelled.
  • Outline quality. OpenStreetMap is volunteer-mapped; outlines vary in age and accuracy.

Reproduce it

The pipeline and site are in one repository. Each step is a short script: download water, measure runs, download fires, observe reservoirs, write the manifest. Adding a region is one entry in a configuration file.

Skimline is an independent project built from open data. It cannot promise or guarantee that the data is always the latest or accurate. It is a planning aid and is not for flight operations.
